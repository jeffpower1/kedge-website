What Kedge keeps track of

The checks that catch problems before they become emergencies

These are the screening tests Canadian guidelines recommend. Kedge keeps track of which ones you're due for, so nothing slips through while you wait for a primary care provider.

Ages 50–74

Colon cancer check

A simple take-home test every two years. Colon cancer caught early is very treatable — caught late, it's a different story.

Women 40+

Breast cancer check

A mammogram every two years. In NL you can already self-refer — most people just don't know that.

Women & people with a cervix

Cervical cancer check

A Pap test every three years. One of the most effective cancer screens in medicine.

Most adults 40+

Diabetes check

A blood test every few years. Millions of Canadians have diabetes or prediabetes and don't know it yet.

All adults

Blood pressure & heart health

Blood pressure, cholesterol, and heart risk. High blood pressure has no symptoms until it does.

Long-time smokers 55–74

Lung cancer check

A low-dose CT scan for people who smoked for many years. It finds lung cancer while it's still small.

Which checks apply to you depends on your age, sex, and health history. That's exactly what Kedge figures out — so you're never guessing, and never getting tests you don't need.

Common questions

Fair questions, straight answers

Is Kedge a replacement for a family doctor?

No, and it's not meant to be. A family physician or nurse practitioner provides full-scope care that no app can. Kedge supports you with one important piece — age-appropriate screening — while you wait to be attached. When you are, your screening history is ready to share with your new provider.

What does it cost?

The plan is for Kedge to be free for patients — funded through the provincial health system rather than out of your pocket. Cost should never be the reason someone skips a cancer screen. We'll confirm the details when we launch in your area.

I already have a family doctor. Is Kedge for me?

Kedge is built first for people without one. If you have a family doctor, keep seeing them — they're your best resource for preventive care.

Who decides which tests I need?

Canadian medical guidelines do — the same ones your family doctor would use, from bodies like the Canadian Task Force on Preventive Health Care and Diabetes Canada. Kedge applies them to your situation; it doesn't invent its own advice.

Is Kedge available right now?

We're launching in Newfoundland and Labrador in stages, working alongside the provincial health system. Join the waitlist and we'll email you as soon as it's available where you live.

What if a test finds something?

Finding something early is the whole point — early almost always means more treatable. If a screening result needs follow-up, Kedge helps make sure that follow-up actually happens, rather than leaving you with a result and no next step.
